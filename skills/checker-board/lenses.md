# The four lenses

The checker's own checklists for a business that is already open. They are not
summaries of any book and speak for no one. A board member applies ONE lens and
reads the evidence in `checker/`, not the owner's hopes.

## The Money lens

Is this business worth the owner's money and time, as it is today?

- **Does it earn, after everything?** Net profit after a fair owner's salary,
  not before it. A business that only "profits" because the owner works for free
  is a job that pays less than it looks.
- **Where does each Rupiah go?** Direct cost, labour, rent and bills, the
  platforms' cut, tax. Which line moved most in the last six months, and why?
- **What does it keep?** After the owner's drawings and the debt payments, does
  cash build up or drain? How many weeks of costs are in the bank?
- **How far above break-even?** A business at 105% of break-even has no room
  for a slow month, a rent rise or a fee change.
- **Return on what is in it.** What the owner put in (the fit-out, the deposit,
  the stock, the unpaid months) against what it pays them a year.

Questions this lens asks: What would the owner earn as an employee doing the
same hours? Which single cost, cut by a quarter, changes the verdict? What is
the cash buffer for Lebaran and THR?

## The Customer lens

Do buyers come back, and why?

- **Repeat or replace?** A business that lives on new buyers each month spends
  to replace the ones it loses. Look at what the reviews and the lapsed customers
  say about why they left.
- **The complaint that costs the most.** Not the loudest one: the one most tied
  to the buyers who stopped.
- **What they praise.** The reason people choose it. A fix that damages this
  makes things worse.
- **The promise against the delivery.** What the menu, the listing or the sign
  promises, against what the reviews describe.

Questions this lens asks: How many of last quarter's buyers bought again? Would
the regulars notice if it closed? What does a 1-star review get wrong, and what
does it get right?

## The Position lens

Can it hold its ground against what is around it now?

- **What changed since opening.** New competitors, a chain moving in, a road or
  an office closing, a platform changing its fees or its ranking.
- **Why this one and not the next one.** If the honest answer is "it is closer"
  or "it is cheaper", the position is weak: anyone can open closer or go
  cheaper.
- **Something hard to copy.** A recipe, a supplier, a location, a community, a
  name people search for. Most small businesses have one; name it, or say there
  is none.
- **Dependence.** On one platform, one supplier, one key staff member, one
  landlord. Each is a risk outside the owner's control.

Questions this lens asks: Who would take its customers if it closed for a month?
What would a competitor need to copy it? What happens if the main platform raises
its commission by five points?

## The Local market lens

What decides whether a business keeps working in Indonesia, from the checker's
reference files (`../checker-compliance/reference/`).

- **The papers.** NIB and KBLI, halal and its deadline, PIRT or BPOM, PBJT. A
  missing one can close a marketplace store or block a bank loan.
- **People.** UMK, BPJS, THR, the staff who do not come back after mudik.
- **Promo culture.** Buyers trained by vouchers and cashback; whether the
  business's own promos pay for themselves or only buy orders at a loss.
- **The calendar.** Ramadan, Lebaran, the rainy season, payday, 11.11 and 12.12.
  Read the months against it before calling a trend.
- **Region.** What works in Jabodetabek and what changes in the business's own
  city: incomes, habits, the informal competitors.

Questions this lens asks: Which missing paper could stop sales tomorrow? Is the
cash ready for THR? Which month of the year carries the business, and which one
drains it?
